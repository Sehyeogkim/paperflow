Inflammation and immune cell dynamics are widely recognized as central drivers of atherosclerotic lesion development and instability. Foundational reviews summarize the roles of innate and adaptive immune mediators, proinflammatory cytokines, and oxidized lipoproteins in atherogenesis and plaque destabilization [cite:hansson2002, cite:frostegrd2013]. Experimental work further links monocyte recruitment and macrophage content to lesion progression and regression in murine models, providing mechanistic context for how changes in lipid load or therapy translate into altered cellular composition of plaques [cite:potteaux2011]. At the level of endothelial biology, interactions between inflammatory cytokines such as IL‑6 and TNF‑α have been implicated in coronary endothelial dysfunction in disease models, supporting a pathway by which systemic or local inflammation can modulate vascular function and possibly plaque behavior [cite:lee2017].

Plaque composition and microstructural features have been associated with clinical vulnerability. Studies of plaque biology emphasize microcalcification as a potential contributor to mechanical instability, and work describing the genesis and growth of extracellular‑vesicle–derived microcalcifications highlights how mineralization processes arise within lesions [cite:hutcheson2016]. Recent clinical and review articles synthesize current perspectives on the vulnerable coronary plaque, placing compositional markers such as lipid pools, fibrous cap status and calcification patterns in the context of rupture risk and clinical management [cite:stefanadis2017]. Imaging‑based studies have also linked wall shear stress (WSS) patterns to plaque burden and composition in patients, suggesting that local hemodynamic forces correlate with—and may help predict—biological plaque features measurable by intravascular imaging [cite:eshtehardi2012].

Complementing the biological literature, a growing biomechanics and computational‑hemodynamics literature connects mechanical loading to plaque natural history. Reviews highlight the role of biomechanical forces in coronary atherosclerosis progression, while targeted studies relate computed metrics such as WSS and axial plaque stress to lesion geometry and composition [cite:brown2016, cite:choi2015]. Patient‑specific and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) analyses have been used to model how stenoses and plaque shapes alter coronary hemodynamics and WSS, providing methodological precedents for linking in vivo imaging with biomechanical metrics [cite:chaichana2012, cite:eshtehardi2012]. Building on these strands, this paper aims to integrate patient‑level CFD‑derived mechanical metrics with localized histopathological markers of inflammation and microcalcification, extending prior associative studies by mapping spatially resolved hemodynamic stresses to cellular and mineral features within coronary plaques.

Interventional context is relevant when considering translational implications: perspectives on evolving devices and therapies remind that mechanical and compositional plaque features together influence revascularization decisions and scaffold design [cite:serruys2011].
