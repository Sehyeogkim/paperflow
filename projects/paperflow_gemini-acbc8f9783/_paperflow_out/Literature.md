Acute coronary syndromes (ACS) are frequently caused by the rupture of atherosclerotic plaques, particularly those with specific morphological characteristics [cite:naghavi2003, cite:shah2003, cite:wal1999]. Research has identified several features associated with vulnerable plaques, including a large necrotic core, thin fibrous cap, and positive remodeling [cite:virmani2005, cite:motoyama2009]. Histopathological studies have shown that plaque rupture often occurs at sites of high circumferential stress [cite:cheng1993]. Clinical guidelines emphasize the management of ACS in patients without persistent ST-segment elevation [cite:roffi2015], and prospective studies have investigated the natural history of coronary atherosclerosis to better understand lesion-related risk factors [cite:stone2011].

Further studies have explored the role of various factors in plaque vulnerability. For instance, cigarette smoking and abnormal serum cholesterol concentrations have been linked to acute coronary thrombosis and sudden death, potentially through plaque rupture or erosion [cite:burke1997]. The thrombogenicity of different atherosclerotic plaque components has also been characterized, providing insights into the consequences of plaque rupture [cite:fernndezortz1994]. Despite these advances, identifying vulnerable plaques before an event remains a significant challenge [cite:naghavi2003].

This paper aims to build upon this body of work by integrating detailed plaque morphology with advanced biomechanical modeling to predict plaque rupture risk. While previous studies have highlighted the importance of stress distribution [cite:cheng1993], our approach incorporates a more comprehensive set of patient-specific and plaque-specific parameters, leveraging computational methods to explore the interplay of these factors. This work contributes to the ongoing effort to better understand and predict the mechanisms underlying ACS.
