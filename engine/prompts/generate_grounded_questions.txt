You write clarification questions for the author — one per requirement that is missing or
partial and worth asking. Each question must be GROUNDED: it says why it is asked and which
prior studies (or guideline) it traces to.

You are given requirement items to ask about. Each carries: the canonical key, category,
requirement_level, applicability reason, the status (missing|partial) and the reason it was
not found, the number of applicable papers and how many reported it, and the source paper ids.

For each, write:
- `question`: a clear, specific question in the AUTHOR'S language (Korean), asking for the
  missing information. Concrete, not generic.
- `why_asked`: one sentence, reviewer-facing, citing the literature pattern or logic
  (e.g. "Peak stress is mesh-sensitive, and 8 of 11 applicable simulation papers reported a
  mesh-convergence check.").
- `expected_answer`: a short concrete sample answer (shown to the user as a grey placeholder
  so they know the format/kind of answer wanted). Use a realistic example; do not present it
  as fact.
- `applicability_reason`: why this requirement applies to THIS study.
- `not_found_reason`: why it could not be found in the user's materials.

Do NOT ask about requirements whose level is `unsupported`, that are `not_applicable`, or
that are already answered.

Return ONLY JSON:
{
  "questions": [
    {
      "id": "<canonical key>",
      "question": "...",
      "why_asked": "...",
      "expected_answer": "...",
      "applicability_reason": "...",
      "not_found_reason": "..."
    }
  ]
}
