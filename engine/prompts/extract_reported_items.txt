You read ONE prior study and extract the methodological and evidentiary items it reports.
Goal: build a picture of what studies LIKE this one conventionally report, so we can later
ask the author for anything they are missing.

You are given the paper's metadata, its content level, and the available text (often only
the abstract).

Rules — read carefully:
- The TOP-LEVEL `category` of each item is FIXED. Use exactly one of:
  objective | study_design | data_or_sample | methods | models_and_equations |
  numerical_or_experimental_settings | validation | outcomes | results |
  statistical_analysis | limitations | figures_and_tables | reported_items
- The item NAME (`raw_name`) and `sub_category` are FREE — name them as the paper does
  (e.g. "mesh independence test", "Mooney–Rivlin constitutive model", "patient inclusion
  criteria", "ablation study", "primary endpoint").
- CONTENT-LEVEL DISCIPLINE (critical):
  - If content_level is "abstract_only", you may extract: objective, study_design (type),
    data_or_sample (broad), primary methods named in the abstract, main outcomes, and main
    results/conclusions. Do NOT assert detailed Method settings (mesh, solver, convergence,
    boundary conditions, hyperparameters) as reported — they are not in an abstract. If you
    infer such a category is *probably relevant*, set explicitly_reported=false and say so
    in description; do NOT fabricate evidence_text.
  - If content_level is "full_text" or "partial_text", extract detailed items where the text
    supports them, with a short evidence_text passage.
- NEVER invent values, numbers, or settings. Missing is missing.
- Do NOT promote one paper's unique implementation detail into a universal requirement —
  just report what THIS paper did.
- Mark `explicitly_reported` true only when the text actually states it.
- `importance_to_main_claim`: high | medium | low | unknown.

Also state the paper's `study_archetype` in a short English phrase.

Return ONLY JSON:

  "study_archetype": "...",
  "content_level": "...",
  "reported_items": [
    {
      "raw_name": "...",
      "category": "<one fixed category>",
      "sub_category": "...",
      "description": "...",
      "section": "",
      "evidence_text": "",
      "importance_to_main_claim": "high|medium|low|unknown",
      "explicitly_reported": true,
      "applies_to": ["..."],
      "source_location": ""
    }
  ]
}
